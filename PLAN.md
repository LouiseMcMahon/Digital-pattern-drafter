# Digital Pattern Drafter — plan

Standing rules: REQUIREMENTS.md (R1 measurements match, R2 extremes, R5 standard tailor pose, R6 no drift) and ENGINEERING.md (test layers, golden snapshots, procedure for changing the model).

See REQUIREMENTS.md for the non-negotiables. In particular: the body must match entered sewing measurements within 0.5 cm, including for extreme bodies (first users are 6'2" women).

A web app: enter measurements (later: a 3D scan) → get a 3D body → draw seam
lines on it → get flat sewing patterns with horizontal and vertical ease,
positive or negative, including stretch fabrics (knit, lycra, latex).

## Decisions
- Web app, TypeScript, three.js, Vite, Vitest. Geometry core is plain TS in `src/core` (no DOM) so it can move to a worker/WASM later.
- First garment: bodice.
- Stretch fabrics are supported just by using negative ease. No fabric profiles: the user chooses the percentage.
- Scope: pull panels off a dress form (e.g. princess-seam bodices), not a full design tool. No darts; shaping comes from seam placement.
- Personal tool that may be published: no SMPL/SMPL-X (non-commercial). Body is a procedural loft built from tape measurements; scan import later registers this same template.

## How ease works
Ease is a percentage applied to the body's own lengths when flattening, independently along horizontal (weft) and vertical (warp):
- scale = 1 + percent / 100, so +5 = 5% larger, -10 = 10% smaller (negative ease for stretch fabric)
- horizontal ease is a smooth field interpolated between knots (bust/waist/hip heights); vertical is a single % for now
- "horizontal/vertical" are weft/warp; per-panel grainline rotates the frame (not yet built)

## Milestones
0. [done] Spike: LSCM unwrap + relaxation toward eased targets, demo page.
1. [done] Body: full torso (front + back) from bust, underbust, waist, hip, chest, neck, shoulder width, bust apex separation/prominence and ring heights. Circumferences are fitted by reading them back off the mesh. Named landmarks (vertex ids). Panels are sub-meshes with boundary loops. Orbit camera in the demo.
2. [done, see limitations] Body v2: full body (head, neck, arms, hands, legs, feet) baked from Anny (tools/bake_body, public/body, 5 MB; 40 PCA components reproduce sampled adults to ~0.07 mm mean). 34 sewing measurements (incl. armscye depth, high bust, across back/chest, shoulder length, shoulder to bust, bust height, waist to hip, crotch depth/length, arm segments) with one shared definition (src/core/measure.ts) used for fitting, read-back and tests. Levenberg-Marquardt fit (src/core/fit.ts) in a Web Worker; tests in src/core/fit.test.ts cover the R1/R2 fixtures: 6'2" woman (average/broad/narrow), petite, plus size, sparse input, implausible input. Every fixture matches within 0.5 cm (typically < 0.1 cm).
3. Seam drawing (in progress, on the baked body). Done: `src/core/seam.ts` with raycast picking, control points stored as face + barycentric (they follow the body when it is re-fitted), snapping to a nearby vertex, shortest surface paths (Dijkstra over mesh edges plus 3 extra points per edge finds the route; `geodesic.ts` then pulls it taut by unfolding the strip of triangles it crosses and running the funnel algorithm, so a seam is as straight as the surface allows and does not depend on how the mesh is triangulated: exact on flat and developable test surfaces, mirror images give equal paths), open and closed seams with lengths, and a click-to-draw mode in the viewer (undo, close, new seam, clear). Snapping and dragging (done): points snap by screen distance (10 px) to the named landmarks (neck points, shoulder points, bust apex, axilla, nape, crotch, hip level; shown as green dots while drawing) and to the ends of other seams, then to a nearby vertex; a control point can be dragged (it snaps while moving) and shift-click deletes it; hidden landmarks do not snap. Panels (done): `src/core/cut.ts` re-triangulates every triangle a seam passes through so the seam lies exactly on mesh edges (surface and area unchanged, checked in tests), then a panel is the region you click inside, found by flood fill that never crosses a seam edge. Each panel is remembered by a point inside it, so it follows the seams and the body; it is tinted on the body, flattened with its own horizontal and vertical ease %, and drawn flat with its size and edge-length error. A region that is not enclosed (over 60% of the body) or has more than one boundary (wraps a limb) is reported instead of flattened. Guides (done): while drawing, the bust, underbust, waist, high bust, high hip and hip lines are shown (teal) with the four points where each crosses centre front, centre back and the sides (`src/core/guides.ts`); clicks snap to those points ("waist line, centre front"), to the lines themselves ("waist line"), to landmarks such as the bust apex (green) and to other seams, and a label next to the pointer says what a click will snap to. A princess seam is then: shoulder point, bust apex, waist line at centre front or at the point below the apex, hip line. Mirror (done): "Mirror last seam" copies the last seam to the other side of the body (`src/core/mirror.ts`); points within 3 mm of the centre line are shared, so the two halves meet exactly. Draw half a bodice from centre front to centre waist, mirror it, click inside: the panel is symmetric (checked in the browser smoke test to within 2% of its width). Several panels (done): every seam is cut together, so panels share seam lines: each panel lists its sides as `Seam n: length (flat length), shared with panel m`, and the seam number is drawn on the flat outline (same number on both panels, so you can check that sewn edges match). A new seam can start or end anywhere along an existing one: the click snaps to its path and the other seam gains a control point there (also when a dragged point is dropped on a seam). Checked in the browser with a bodice front and back from 12 seams. Finishing touches (done): Undo/Redo (`history.ts`, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z) cover every change to the seams and panels, including drags, deletes, mirror, merge, panel ease; the design is saved to a file and opened again (`design.ts`: versioned JSON, checked against the mesh, a bad file changes nothing) and also kept in the browser so a reload keeps it; seams that cross or end on each other are joined at a shared control point automatically (`crossings.ts`; a seam is not joined to itself); cutting and flattening run in a worker (`panelPipeline.ts`, `panels.worker.ts`); the flat piece shows each seam's number and flat length and a dashed seam allowance line (`allowance.ts`, mitred corners, default 1 cm, 0 turns it off). Not yet: a one-click princess seam (deliberately left out: people draw their own), grainline per panel (milestone 4), a seam that crosses itself, undo of a fit.
4. Flattening for arbitrary panels: per-panel grainline, faster/more exact solver (ARAP / Gauss-Newton), distortion heatmap.
5. Fit checks and export: paired seam-length comparison, notches, seam allowance, SVG/DXF/tiled PDF at 1:1.
6. Drape preview (optional): cloth sim of sewn panels on the body.
7. Scan import: PLY/OBJ, non-rigid registration of the template.

## Draped panels (in progress)
A panel is not the skin: cloth is pinned at its seams and pulled taut between them. `src/core/drape.ts`: `drapeSeams` pulls every seam taut between its control points (cut.pins) so it stands off the skin across hollows (cleavage, spine); `drape` then relaxes each panel's inside toward the smoothest surface spanning those seams, never below the skin (each vertex moves along its own skin normal, height >= standoff). The draped mesh is what gets flattened and what the 3D view shows; shared seams stay identical on both panels. On a centre front panel cut by princess seams through the bust, worst strain drops from 3.6% to 2.1%; a whole front with no seam over the bust barely changes (13%), because its boundary still wraps the bust. Not yet: standoff/ease as a distance from the body, limbs as obstacles, free (off-body) edges such as skirt hems, running in the worker, mutation-testing entry for drape.ts. Next: skirts (panel boundary with a design curve off the body, e.g. hem).

### Cloth simulation (opt-in in the app)
`src/core/cloth.ts` (`drapeCloth`) lays the flat pattern over the body: position-based dynamics with every edge held to its pattern length (no stretch), bending resistance across edges, the body as an obstacle (`bodyField.ts`, grid closest-point), and the border drawn toward its taut-seam targets with a pull that eases off, so what remains is the border as close as it can get without stretching. `clothPattern` then flattens the cloth itself and returns the pair (cloth, its own pattern) with the stretch between them, after one short extra simulate-and-reflatten round: measured against the membrane's pattern the cloth showed 1-2% left over, but the cloth is close to developable and its own flattening needs far less. Shoulder princess front: centre 3.4% (skin) to 0.14%, side 9.5% to 0.56% (seams up to 0.9 cm short); whole front with no bust seam 13% to 1.1% (2.8 cm short). More edge passes and a weaker border pull lower the strain further but let the seams fall further short. Takes 3-10 s per panel, so the app runs it only on the 'Simulate cloth' button (in `src/cloth.worker.ts`, so the page stays responsive and shows progress) and keeps the result until the panel's shape changes. Bending (default 0.01) holds opposite corners at their flat distance, which leans the cloth toward flat; 0, 0.01 and 0.05 give the same lift and strain to within about 0.3 cm and 0.4%, and needs more edge passes or a better solver for the last percent. Not yet: gravity-driven pieces (skirts), limbs as obstacles.

## Known limitations
- Measurement definitions are mine (documented in measure.ts) and have NOT been calibrated against real tape measurements. Two known proxies: across back/chest use the arm skinning weight at 0.8 (CREASE_WEIGHT), chosen so an average woman lands in published ranges; armscye depth carries a +4 cm CALIBRATION estimated from published means (the mesh underarm is higher than a tailor's ruler level). Real people's numbers (especially shoulder width, underbust, nape-to-waist, front length) may differ systematically from these definitions. Next step: measure a real person and compare.
- Not yet measurable: armscye circumference (the mesh has no arm/torso boundary line to measure around), sitting crotch depth, outseam along the surface, back/front neck drop, and separate left/right limbs (L/R are averaged).
- The tailor pose (R5) is arms 30 degrees from vertical (A-pose) with straight elbows, vertical legs and flat feet; head and spine are left as the model has them. Other poses (seated, arms raised for sleeve checks) are not implemented.
- Fixtures are plausible invented measurement sets, not real people. Replace/add real sets as they become available.
- The fit uses only local controls that change size and proportion (FIT_CONTROL in src/core/fit.ts); posture-like morphs are excluded because they hit numbers by distorting the body.
- The earlier ring-loft torso (src/core/body.ts and the flatten tests that use it) is superseded by the baked body and will be removed once flattening runs on the new mesh. The demo page no longer shows panels/ease; that returns with seam drawing.
- Flattening: LSCM start, then as-rigid-as-possible (local-global) refinement toward the eased edge lengths (`flatten.ts`). It does not depend on how the mesh is triangulated, and a mirror-symmetric panel comes out symmetric to about 2 mm mean / 6 mm worst on a front bodice (the remainder is the discretisation). A curved surface cannot lie flat, so something has to give. Seams give nothing: border edges are held to their true (eased) length, so a seam shared by two panels is the same length on both (within 0.4 mm on a front/back bodice, checked by `panels.integration.test.ts` and in the browser), and the strain goes into the inside of the panel. That strain is not acceptable in a finished pattern (REQUIREMENTS.md R7: no stretch, 0.5% at most): `worstStrain` is checked, and a piece over the limit (a front bodice over the bust is about 4-5%) is reported as unable to lie flat, with a red/blue strain map on the body and on the flat piece and the advice to add a dart or more seam lines. Not yet: suggesting where. About 0.5 s for a 1500-vertex panel (edge-list Laplacian built once, Jacobi-preconditioned CG); it still runs on the main thread when panels are drawn.
- A fit takes a few seconds in a worker; the Jacobian is finite-difference.
- Landmarks: the shoulder and neck points come from the live surface and are stable (a 1 degree arm change used to move shoulder width 1.8 cm; now 0.0). The bust apex, underarm, crotch, hip level and nape are still vertices chosen once on the mean body and can move by about a vertex spacing (~1 cm) when upstream geometry shifts; bounded by measure.stability.test.ts. Exact surface points would remove that.
- No real-person measurement data yet: everything is checked against published ranges and against itself.
- High bust vs bust: in this model the chest above the breast is nearly as wide as at the apex, so an average woman's high bust is only ~2 cm under her bust (published data: 5-7 cm). Entering a large bust/high-bust difference may leave one of the two unmet. Needs real-person data to confirm and then a better breast/chest shape or ring height.
- Underbust is defined as a fixed fraction below the bust apex. On a large, overhanging bust the plane can slip into the lower breast and the girth jumps (seen: 109 -> 125 cm at an extreme control value). A definition that finds the inframammary fold would be better; it needs real data to validate.
- Arms are held a fixed 30 degrees from vertical. A full 90-degree T-pose was tried and breaks underarm detection (the arms never merge with the trunk, so the bisection in `detectLandmarks` finds nothing). On wide bodies the arms can touch or pass through the trunk at the waist and hip, which merges loops in the ring slices; the trunk-ring code falls back to a torso-only slice when that happens. A pose that guarantees clearance (angle chosen per body) would be more robust.
